In the evolving digital age, technology has significantly changed the way corrupt activities are revealed. It helps people share information more easily, makes illegal activities harder to hide through digital records, allows early detection of fraud using artificial intelligence and even ensure data transparency without trust any central authority.

To begin with, information is easier to share nowadays. Social media platforms, like Facebook, are public. Everyone can publish and share information, making it harder to hide corruption or scandals. Even when there are some parties trying to censor content, the information has already been leaked, so they cannot completely hide it.

Another reason is digital actions often leave trails. Most online activities, like payments, leave data records, which investigators can use to trace digital records. For example, banks know the money flow in a client's bank account, who transfers money to an account and to whom the money is sent to. In this way, money laundering can be detected and limited.

In the era of AI, fraud detections use artificial intelligence to identify illegal activities even in the early stage. AI systems can analyze a large amount of data, such as transactions or online behaviors, much faster than humans. By learning and recognizing unusual patterns, companies or AND governments are able to detect fraud early and prevent unintended consequences.

However, an important question remains that who supervises the supervisors? To solve that problem, trustless platforms, especially blockchain, are developed and evolving. With blockchain technology, users do not need to trust banks, governments or any central authority. Transaction records are publicly available and shared among at least two thirds of available participants in a blockchain network. If someone tries to alter or fake a transaction, other participants still hold the correct data and can detect fraud. In that way, transparency is ensured in trustless and decentralized platforms.

In conclusion, technology plays a crucial role in exposing corruption. Since corruption is becoming increasingly sophisticated over time, technology must continue to evolve to detect hidden activities and increase transparency to protect public trust.
