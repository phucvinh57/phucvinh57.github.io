One of the most impressive history books that I have read is Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind by Yuvah Noah HARARI. It is a deep exploration of human history, from the emergence of early Homo Sapiens to the technological and social developments shaping the modern world. To discover it, let us go through three big revolutions in human history, first, the cognitive revolution, second, the agricultural revolution and, third, the scientific revolution.

The cognitive revolution, which occurred about 70,000 years ago, marked a starting point in human history by giving Homo sapiens a much more flexible language than other animals. With complex language ability, humans can share detailed information about environments, plan group hunting strategies and transmit knowledge across generations, which dramatically increases survival. Another major function of human language is gossip. It helps humans live in larger social groups. Knowing who can be trusted or not helps maintain cooperation, especially in hunting. Social hierarchy appeared, in which individuals who gained the most reputation would become leaders and keep their groups stable. However, human groups reached a maximum capacity of 200 individuals. If a group grew bigger than 200 members, there would be a fight, then it would splinter. The most important function of human language is the ability of believing in abstract things, like myths. Therefore, when various people believe in the same myth, they can cooperate with other groups and strangers. With these language gifts, Homo sapiens are able to organize, plan, and collaborate on a larger scale that gives a competitive advantage over other human species, such as Neanderthals, eventually leading to the global dominance of Homo sapiens.

The second major transformation in human history is the agricultural revolution, which began about 12,000 years ago. During this period, humans shifted from a nomadic lifestyle of hunting and gathering to farming and domesticating plants and animals. Theoretically, this change allowed people to produce more food and support larger populations. However, HARARI raises a controversial argument that agriculture is a contract with the devil. Homo sapiens domesticated wheat, but,  in contrast, he thinks wheat has domesticated us. We chose agriculture, but it required us to settle down and spend most of our time on farms and working harder than the hunting lifestyle before. During this period, the term “future” appeared. In the case of good weather and environments, we would have bumper harvests, but how about the cases of bad ones? To cope with these uncertain times, people needed to store food and plan ahead. However, they also need to protect their food from thieves and violent attacks. They started to pay a portion of their harvests to those who provided protection. It led to the emergence of governments. They were created to protect and help people, but in reality they often dominated them, forcing people to work harder while receiving less in return. Therefore, agriculture has totally changed humankind. After we realized it, several centuries had passed and no one remembers how we had lived before. There is no going back. Wheat, pigs and sheep dominate us, rather than humans dominating them, according to Harari.

The third transformation in human history, over the last 500 years, is the scientific revolution. Compared to the two previous ones, it has been much shorter in duration but has changed the world at an unprecedented pace. Suppose a farmer had slept for 500 years since the 1000s then woke up, he would not have seen much change. However, if he had slept since the 1500s, then woke up in the 21th century, he would find himself in a strange world and might ask , “is it heaven or hell”? He would see tall buildings rising like mountains of glasses, small shining boxes in people’s hands, which can talk, and a lot of metal horses running on the roads. In the 15th century, the sky belonged to birds, angels and the gods. On July 20, 1969, humans put their first step on the moon. Why has the third transformation totally changed the world? There are three main differences between modern science versus all previous knowledge systems. First, people are ready to admit their ignorance. The current knowledge we know may be wrong, if we have more information. There are few concepts, ideologies or theories that are true forever. Second, after admitting ignorance, people place observation and experimentation at the center of knowledge. Theories are built on the previous observations and experimental results. Third, modern science is never not satisfied with only creating theories, but always tries to apply them. In fact, the scientific revolution is not a revolution of knowledge, but a revolution of ignorance. For example, when a person in a previous knowledge system wondered where humans came from, he might ask a priest without verification. However, nowadays, even Darwin might say, “I don’t know”, if we ask him the same question. The theory of evolution can be wrong. After reading this idea, I seriously reflected on myself. Am I blindly believing something I was taught or experienced? Only by doing so can I truly think like a scientist and continue to learn in an ever-changing world. We should not blindly believe something we were taught or experienced. Only by doing so can we truly think like a scientist and continue to learn in an ever-changing world.

In conclusion, Sapiens does not simply tell the story of human history but also challenges the way I see the world. There are a lot of interesting view points and questions raised by the author, which I cannot fully express here. HARARI tells the story from a detached, impersonal perspective, focusing on large-scale patterns and systems. When I have more time, I will carefully read it again.
